# Threads: Böcklin’s *Isle of the Dead* and what it set off

Research for the reel “Threads” (variant D): a chain of real influences, each step a reference to
a reference. Checked October 2026 against Wikipedia, museum and orchestra programme notes; sources
are under each step. **Confidence:** ✅ several independent sources agree · ⚠️ one kind of source or
a disputed detail; the reel words it carefully.

No nude figures appear in any of the works below.

## The main thread

| Year | Place | Work | Link to the previous step | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1872 | Munich | Böcklin, *Self-Portrait with Death Playing the Fiddle* | Death as a fiddler comes from the medieval Dance of Death (Freund Hein) | ✅ |
| 1880 | Florence | Böcklin, the first *Isle of the Dead* | A widow, Marie Berna, saw it half-finished and asked for “a picture to dream over”; for her version he added the coffin and the figure in white | ✅ |
| 1883 | Berlin | Dealer Fritz Gurlitt names it *Die Toteninsel* | Böcklin never gave it the title | ✅ |
| 1880–86 | | Five painted versions | Basel, New York (Met), Berlin, Leipzig; the fourth burned in WWII | ✅ |
| 1890 | | Max Klinger’s black-and-white etching after it | Prints spread; Nabokov (*Despair*, 1936): “found in every Berlin home” | ✅ |
| 1907 | Paris | Rachmaninoff sees a black-and-white reproduction | Which print it was is not known | ✅ (print unknown ⚠️) |
| 1909 | Dresden → Moscow | Rachmaninoff, *Isle of the Dead*, Op. 29 | Opens in 5/8, like oars; quotes the Dies irae. Later, on the colour original: if he had seen it first, he might not have written the piece (wording varies by source) | ✅ |
| 1907–08 | Stockholm | Strindberg, *The Ghost Sonata* | Ends as the house vanishes and the Isle of the Dead appears; copies of Böcklin’s pictures flanked his stage | ✅ |
| 1913 | | Reger, *Four Tone Poems after Böcklin*, No. 3 *Die Toteninsel* | | ✅ |
| 1932 | (Wuppertal) | Dalí, *The True Painting of “The Isle of the Dead” by Arnold Böcklin at the Hour of the Angelus* | Ties Böcklin to Dalí’s other obsession, Millet’s *The Angelus* (1857–59) | ✅ |

### Side threads

- **Death plays the fiddle.** Böcklin’s self-portrait (1872) → Mahler, Symphony No. 4 (1900), scherzo
  “Freund Hein spielt auf”: the solo violin is tuned a whole tone up. Alma Mahler wrote that Mahler was
  “under the spell” of the self-portrait ✅ (her account only). In parallel, Saint-Saëns, *Danse macabre*
  (1874): Death’s fiddle with the E string tuned down to E♭ ✅.
- **Millet → Dalí.** Dalí was sure *The Angelus* hid a child’s coffin; at his urging the Louvre X-rayed it
  (1963 in one source) and found a dark shape by the basket. That it is a coffin is Dalí’s reading ⚠️.
- **Lewton** had already hung the painting on a wall in *I Walked with a Zombie* (1943) ✅.

### Not used

- Hitler owned a version: true, but sources disagree on details and it is not a thread worth pulling here.
- Giger’s *Alien* designs drawing directly on the Böcklin homage: no source says so.
- *Shutter Island* (2010) echoing the 1945 film: one source only.
